Audience Terminology Map

Purpose #

This page translates Before We Build research language for two public audiences:

  1. a Christian audience, especially readers who expect Scripture-first and pastorally careful language;
  2. a general audience seeking responsible conversation, relationship, role, or team guidance.

It does not replace Core Glossary or Extended Research Glossary. Those pages define research terms. This page governs the first public explanation of them.

Governing Rule #

Research terms describe a proposed architecture. Public language should describe what a person can responsibly observe, discuss, and decide next.

For Christian readers, lead toward Scripture, character, prayer, wisdom, church community, honest conversation, and mature counsel. For a general audience, lead toward clarity, dignity, consent, safety, evidence, responsibility, and human judgment.

No public term may turn a typological hypothesis into an identity verdict, moral rank, safety decision, or promised relationship outcome.

Four-Level Translation #

BWB level Christian public question General public question
Value-moral foundation What are we building before God, and what do character, conduct, consent, safety, responsibility, reciprocity, and repair show? Which values, obligations, conduct, boundaries, consent, safety, and repair practices matter here?
Strategic / Temporistics Where are we going, and how do memory, hope, fear, direction, and meaning shape us? How do our temporal horizons and meaning frames interact?
Operational / Psychosophy How do we choose, reason, express feelings, carry responsibility, and care for practical life? How do we organize joint action across agency, reasoning, emotion, body, and resources?
Tactical / Socionics How do we speak, listen, exchange information, misunderstand, and repair? How do we model and exchange information in this situation?

Context is a condition across all four levels. Safety is a mandatory constraint, not another level.

Socionics Terms #

  • Model A, information elements, function positions, quadras, and traditional relation names belong in the research route.
  • In public guidance, translate them into questions about salient information, wording, misunderstanding, and repair.
  • Do not infer intelligence, empathy, morality, aggression, or fate from an element or position.

Psychosophy Terms #

  • Will → agency, responsibility, initiative, and pressure around deciding; not spiritual maturity.
  • Logic → reasoning and explanation; not wisdom or truthfulness.
  • Emotion → priority and organization of emotional expression; not love or moral fruit.
  • Physics → body, comfort, resources, home, and practical care; not materialism.
  • Positions describe hypothesized modes, not ranks of human value.

Temporistics Terms #

  • Past → memory, continuity, story, wounds, gratitude, and learning.
  • Present → place, belonging, attention, and current contact.
  • Future → direction, planning, hope, and fear.
  • Eternity → meaning and ultimate frame; in Christian public copy, explain the research usage so it is not mistaken for doctrine.
  • Archetype names remain optional research labels and should not become identities.

AI Boundary #

AI may assist only with low-authority support tasks such as:

  • summarizing material the user has chosen to provide;
  • organizing the user's own notes and questions;
  • suggesting alternative questions or explanations for human review;
  • keeping sources, caveats, and unresolved uncertainty visible.

AI must never be presented as a simulated person, a substitute for another person's participation, a predictor of an individual or relationship, a spiritual authority, or an automated compatibility judge. It cannot infer consent, safety, character, truthfulness, or another person's future behavior from a profile.

Preferred Human Questions #

Replace labels with questions whenever the label is not necessary:

  • What did we observe directly, and what are we interpreting?
  • What other explanations fit the same behavior?
  • Which level does this question belong to?
  • What context, power difference, consent, or safety issue changes the reading?
  • What should we ask the other person instead of predicting them?
  • What evidence would weaken this hypothesis?
  • Which decision still belongs to the people involved, with appropriate counsel?

Editorial Workflow #

  1. Identify the source-layer term and its claim status.
  2. Decide whether a first-time reader needs the technical term at all.
  3. If not, replace it with a concrete human question.
  4. If yes, define it briefly, name the source system, and state what cannot be inferred.
  5. Preserve the distinction between direct observation, interpretation, hypothesis, and normative guidance.
  6. Add Scripture grounding near Christian normative claims and request pastoral review where doctrine or church authority is implicated.
  7. Check that AI language stays within the low-authority boundary above.

Strong Non-Claims #

Before We Build does not claim to reveal a person's essence, determine morality, guarantee compatibility, diagnose a relationship, calculate a current pair score, predict another person, or replace Scripture, prayer, consent, safety practice, mature counsel, professional care, or human judgment.
