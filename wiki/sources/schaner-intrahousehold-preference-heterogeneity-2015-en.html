Do Opposites Detract? Intrahousehold Preference Heterogeneity and Inefficient Strategic Savings

Author: Simone Schaner Venue: American Economic Journal: Applied Economics, 2015, 7 (2), pp. 135–174 URL: https://doi.org/10.1257/app.20130271 Language: English

Key Point for Before We Build #

Differences between spouses in intertemporal preferences (time discount rates) cause systematic inefficiencies in household savings and welfare. Mismatched time horizons generate strategic mistrust and costly financial hiding, whereas well-matched couples maximize collective financial gains. This provides causal field-experimental proof for BWB's strategic level (reconstructed Temporistics: temporal framing and horizon coordination).

Main Content of the Article #

Through a randomized field experiment in Kenya involving married couples offered individual and joint bank accounts with randomized interest rates, the author tested how preference heterogeneity impacts economic coordination:

  1. Strategic Inefficiency: Couples with divergent discount rates (one patient, one impatient) frequently sacrificed high joint returns to retain individual control over money.
  2. Mistrust and Precautionary Hiding: Impatient spouses sought immediate liquidity, while patient spouses attempted to lock funds away, resulting in strategic non-cooperation.
  3. Cooperative Gains in Aligned Pairs: Couples with matched intertemporal preferences responded efficiently to interest rate incentives, maximizing total household welfare.

Research Conclusions #

  1. Temporal coordination is not an abstract philosophical preference, but an operational requirement for household survival and material thriving.
  2. When partners operate on conflicting time horizons without explicit agreements, strategic hiding and defensive disengagement follow.
  3. The study validates the necessity of BWB's strategic level while refuting simplistic claims that no preference differences matter.

Source Assessment #

What the Source Claims #

The author claims that heterogeneity in spouses' intertemporal discount rates causes inefficient financial decisions, strategic hiding of savings, and reduced household welfare.

Data or Evidence Provided #

Randomized controlled field experiment with married couples in rural Kenya, tracking transaction-level data across individual and joint bank accounts under varying randomized interest rates.

Limitations #

The study examined monetary discount rates in a developing-economy rural context; cultural norms, legal property rights, and gender asymmetries substantially shape household bargaining and cannot be reduced purely to psychological traits.

What BWB Accepts #

BWB accepts that differences in time preferences and temporal horizons can generate severe relational and material friction, requiring conscious negotiation before major joint commitments.

What Remains Contested or Open #

Whether economic time discount rates map onto the four existential aspects of Temporistics (Past, Present, Future, Eternity) remains an unverified hypothesis.

Rejected or Historical-Only #

BWB rejects assuming that different time preferences make a relationship impossible, or using economic discount rates to assign human or moral worth.
