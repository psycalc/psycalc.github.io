Machine learning uncovers the most robust self-report predictors of relationship quality across 43 longitudinal couples studies

Authors: Samantha Joel, Paul W. Eastwick, Colleen J. Allison, ... Eli J. Finkel Venue: Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences (PNAS), 2020, 117 (32), pp. 19061–19071 URL: https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1917036117 Language: English

Key Point for Before We Build #

In a dataset of 11,196 couples, machine learning models demonstrated that individual trait profiles and trait-matching combinations explain virtually zero incremental variance in dyadic relationship satisfaction. Relationship-specific dynamic variables (perceived commitment, responsiveness, conflict handling) account for up to 45% of variance. This provides landmark empirical validation for BWB's total rejection of static compatibility scores and dating-matchmaker algorithms based on individual typological profiles.

Main Content of the Article #

1. Relationship Variables Versus Individual Traits #

The study applied Random Forest models to 43 longitudinal datasets with 2,278 self-report predictors. Relationship-specific variables—specifically perceived partner commitment, appreciation, sexual satisfaction, perceived partner responsiveness, and conflict frequency—predicted up to ~45% of baseline relationship satisfaction. Individual differences (Big Five traits, self-esteem, attachment styles) explained ~21% of an individual's own satisfaction and had near-zero predictive power for their partner's satisfaction.

2. Failure of Trait Combination Algorithms #

A central question was whether specific combinations of partners' traits predict relationship quality (the core assumption of algorithmic matchmaking). The models revealed that partner interaction terms and dyadic trait matching contributed negligible additional predictive value. Static personality compatibility formulas failed to predict who is happy with whom.

3. Unpredictability of Longitudinal Change #

While current relationship satisfaction is substantially predictable from existing relationship dynamics, changes in relationship quality over time were largely unpredictable from baseline self-reports alone. Relational trajectories depend on emergent interactions, external stressors, and dynamic adaptations rather than static baseline variables.

Research Conclusions #

  1. Compatibility cannot be measured as a static score derived from individual personality inventories.
  2. The empirical literature decisively favors process-oriented, conversational, and relational frameworks over trait-matching algorithms.
  3. BWB typologies (Socionics, Psychosophy, Temporistics) must remain heuristic models of latent coordination processes, never used as predictive matchmaking filters.

Source Assessment #

What the Source Claims #

The authors claim that relationship quality is primarily driven by relationship-specific constructs rather than individual traits, and that machine learning algorithms cannot identify viable partner-matching trait combinations.

Data or Evidence Provided #

Analysis of 43 longitudinal couple datasets comprising 11,196 couples across multiple countries, using cross-validated Random Forest models on 2,278 candidate predictors.

Limitations #

The study relied on self-report questionnaires rather than behavioral observation or physiological tracking; the majority of samples were Western, educated, and demographically homogeneous; longitudinal waves varied in duration across datasets.

What BWB Accepts #

BWB accepts that individual trait inventories do not predict dyadic compatibility, and that relationship quality emerges from relational processes, communication, and mutual commitment.

What Remains Contested or Open #

Whether qualitative, multi-level conversation mapping (such as BWB) can improve real-world discernment and relationship outcomes beyond standard relationship education remains an empirical hypothesis to be tested.

Rejected or Historical-Only #

BWB rejects commercial matchmaking claims that calculate compatibility percentages from personality tests, treating such practices as empirically invalidated by this benchmark.
