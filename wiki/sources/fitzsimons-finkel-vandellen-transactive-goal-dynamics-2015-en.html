Transactive Goal Dynamics

Transactive Goal Dynamics #

Authors: Grainne M. Fitzsimons, Eli J. Finkel, Michelle R. vanDellen

Venue: Psychological Review, 2015, 122 (4), pp. 648–673

URL: https://doi.org/10.1037/a0039654

Language: English

Key Point for Before We Build #

In close relationships, self-regulation and goal pursuit are not individual intrapsychic functions, but form an interdependent transactive goal system. Partners function as a coupled regulatory unit where goals, volitional effort, and tasks are dynamically allocated across individuals. This provides robust conceptual grounding for investigating BWB's operational level (interdependence of regulation, effort allocation, and action organization), while the specific four-aspect partition of Afanasyev remains a BWB research heuristic rather than a direct finding of TGD.

Main Content of the Article #

The authors propose Transactive Goal Dynamics (TGD) theory, advancing relationship science beyond individualistic models of motivation:

1. System Components: Goal pursuit spans self-oriented goals (A for A), partner-oriented goals (A for B), and system-oriented goals (joint goals for the dyad).

2. Regulatory Interdependence: Self-regulatory failure or success in one partner directly depletes or restores the volitional and cognitive resources of the other.

3. Coordination Costs and Asymmetries: Specialization and goal pooling yield efficiency, but poor coordination leads to resentment, perceived free-riding, and systemic exhaustion.

4. Agency vs. Dependence Trade-off: High transactive efficiency can weaken individual self-regulatory capacity if agency is entirely outsourced to a partner.

Research Conclusions #

1. Operational compatibility depends on the mutual negotiation of goal allocation and regulatory support, not on matching personality profiles.

2. Individual volition operates in a social matrix; agency in a couple is distributed and shared.

3. TGD explains task specialization and motivational conflicts without requiring metaphysical typological constructs.

Source Assessment #

What the Source Claims #

The authors claim that goal pursuit and self-regulation in interdependent relationships must be analyzed as a transactive, single-system dynamic where individual agency and dyadic coordination are mutually constitutive.

Data or Evidence Provided #

Broad theoretical synthesis integrating cognitive psychology, social psychology, neuroscience of executive function, and behavioral economics of household bargaining.

Limitations #

The article is a comprehensive theoretical framework rather than a single empirical study; testing transactive networks requires multi-level longitudinal and observational designs.

What BWB Accepts #

BWB accepts that operational action, willpower, and executive coordination in couples are transactive and interdependent, and that failure at this level can destabilize otherwise aligned partnerships.

What Remains Contested or Open #

Whether Afanasyev's four functions (Logic, Physics, Volition, Emotion) validly capture the primary fault lines of transactive goal allocation remains an unverified research hypothesis.

Rejected or Historical-Only #

BWB rejects viewing willpower or operational competence as an innate, unchangeable type trait, or using Psychosophy to justify exploitative division of labor.
