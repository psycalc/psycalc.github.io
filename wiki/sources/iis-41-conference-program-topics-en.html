Before We Build-Authored Summaries #

Before We Build summary: Socionics, MBTI, and Big Five #

The conference program lists a talk by A. V. Bukalov comparing Socionics, Jungian typology, MBTI, and Big Five in terms of claimed psychophysiological correlates.

Safe use: cite this as evidence that IIS/Bukalov is discussing a comparative typology-and-baseline question. Do not cite the program as evidence that Socionics or MBTI have established psychophysiological correlates, or that Big Five lacks such correlates.

Before We Build summary: Improving Typing Accuracy #

The program lists a talk by Yu. P. Minaev on improving typing accuracy as practitioners master additional bipolar type traits.

Safe use: cite this as evidence that typing accuracy and additional diagnostic traits are active topics in the IIS conference ecosystem. Do not cite the program as proof that any specific trait set improves accuracy without a separate article, method, dataset, or validation study.

Before We Build summary: AI Hallucinations from a Socionics Model Perspective #

The program lists a talk by A. V. Bukalov on the problem of AI hallucinations from the perspective of a Socionics model.

Safe use: cite this as evidence that some Socionics authors are beginning to apply Socionics concepts to AI behavior and LLM reliability problems. Do not cite the program as evidence that Socionics explains AI hallucinations unless a substantive paper or dataset is available.

Before We Build Relevance #

These topics are relevant to Before We Build because they touch three project concerns:

  • comparison of Socionics with mainstream personality baselines such as Big Five;
  • reliability and calibration of typing procedures;
  • use and limits of typological models when interpreting AI behavior.

For Before We Build, they should be treated as topic signals and research leads, not as validated findings.

Use Policy #

  • OK: cite talk titles, speakers, conference name, date caveat, and URL.
  • OK: summarize in Before We Build's own words as above.
  • Avoid: copying the full program, screenshots, page layout, or extended descriptions.
  • Avoid: turning talk titles into established empirical claims.
  • If full theses/proceedings appear later, ingest them as separate sources with their own rights and evidence review.

Source Assessment #

What the Source Claims #

The conference program shows which Socionics and adjacent topics organizers and presenters placed on the IIS-41 agenda.

Data or Evidence Provided #

The evidence is the attributed program, titles, speakers, and session metadata; a listing does not itself supply study methods or results.

Limitations #

Conference inclusion is neither peer review nor validation, and abstracts or titles can overstate what a presentation demonstrates.

What BWB Accepts #

BWB accepts the program as provenance, a map of research claims, and a lead for locating primary materials.

What Remains Contested or Open #

The scientific quality, methods, findings, and relevance of individual presentations remain open until their full materials are reviewed.

Rejected or Historical-Only #

BWB rejects treating presence in the program, speaker status, or institutional affiliation as empirical support for a BWB claim.
