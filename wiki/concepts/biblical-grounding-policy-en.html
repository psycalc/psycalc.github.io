Biblical Grounding Policy

Scope #

This policy governs the Christian Before We Build application, not the universal research core. When an application page makes a Christian normative or theological claim, it should show the relevant biblical grounding nearby. A Bible citation does not turn a project hypothesis into empirical evidence and does not make the Christian application the definition of BWB as a whole.

The universal core states its value-moral commitments and evidence limits directly. The Christian application identifies Scripture as its primary normative source and may also cite theological, historical, or pastoral interpretation where that improves traceability.

Biblical grounding for this policy: 2 Timothy 3:16–17; Acts 17:11; 1 Thessalonians 5:21.

Claims requiring biblical grounding #

Grounding is required when a page says what Christians should believe, value, refuse, prioritize, or practise, including claims about marriage, family, faithfulness, forgiveness, sin, church, pastoral counsel, or the spiritual limits of typology.

Examples include:

  • Christian love includes truthfulness, responsibility, patience, and self-giving (1 Corinthians 13:4–7; Ephesians 5:1–2).
  • Forgiveness and repair matter in Christian relationships (Colossians 3:12–14; Ephesians 4:31–32).
  • Human profiles must not become spiritual rank or partiality (1 Samuel 16:7; Romans 12:3; James 2:1).
  • Counsel should serve wise discernment rather than control (Proverbs 11:14; 2 Corinthians 4:2).

The paragraph should explain the connection instead of presenting a bare verse list.

Claims not established by a Bible citation #

Technical definitions, page structure, source attribution, empirical observations, psychometric claims, and research hypotheses require their appropriate evidence. Scripture may explain why the Christian application cares about truth or human dignity, but it does not by itself establish a reliability estimate, a causal mechanism, or a typological mapping.

Use these distinctions:

Statement kind Required support
Christian normative or theological Scripture in context; interpretation source when material
project definition explicit BWB definition and scope
empirical or historical relevant data or attributed source
research hypothesis hypothesis label, alternatives, and falsification conditions
practical application guidance scope, safety limits, and its normative basis

Minimal format #

**Christian application claim:** ...

**Biblical grounding:**

- **Book chapter:verses** — how the passage bears on the claim.

**Interpretive limit:** what the passage does not establish, or where interpretation is contested.

For a disputed or high-impact claim, add the passage context and an attributed interpretive source. More citations do not compensate for weak exegesis.

Limits #

Biblical grounding does not settle every interpretive dispute, replace exegesis, validate a typology, guarantee an outcome, or authorize coercion. Forgiveness language must not be used to hide abuse or remove safety boundaries. When Christians disagree about an application, the page should state the contested point and avoid presenting one inference as the text itself.
