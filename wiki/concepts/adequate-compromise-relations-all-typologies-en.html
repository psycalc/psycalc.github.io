Compromise and Type Relations Across Three Typologies

Compromise and Type Relations Across Three Typologies #

No proposed type relation by itself makes compromise possible, impossible, safe, or desirable. This page translates relation names into qualitative questions: a possible resource, a possible friction, and what must be checked by observation.

Before typological interpretation, apply the truth, commitment, consent, safety, reciprocity, and feasibility boundaries in adequate-compromise-en.

1. Unit of analysis #

An individual type result concerns one person. A relation is a hypothesis about interaction between two results. Even reliable typing would not make it an observation of the pair. Testing requires concrete tasks, both directions of interaction, context, behaviour over time, and outcomes.

2. Socionics relations #

Traditional name Possible resource to investigate Possible friction What to observe
Duality different information contributions dependence on habitual role division accuracy of the shared model and ability to change roles
Activation energy and rapid engagement overload and insufficient pauses decision quality after sustained load

Semi-duality partial complementarity expectation of complete understanding which needs remain unsupported
Business practical task division functionality without closeness role clarity and both people's satisfaction
Kindred similar leading focus duplication and shared gaps quality of outside compensation
Mirage ease in part of the contact weak support under pressure accuracy and repair in difficult tasks
Quasi-identity similar vocabulary different meanings for the same words explicit agreement on criteria
Extinguishment mutual correction of momentum loss of pace ability to work separately and integrate results
Benefit/request directed task transmission asymmetric expectations right to refuse and power distribution
Supervision/revision close checking chronic correction of the other voluntariness, distance, and response to feedback
Superego contrasting representations fatigue from mutual correction benefit of an explicit protocol

Names and mechanisms in the table belong to a typological tradition; BWB has not established their predictive performance.

3. Psychosophy and Temporistics #

For positional systems, describing contact direction is more useful than assigning a quality category to the pair.

Contact pattern Possible resource Possible friction Check
similar positions recognition of approach shared gaps or competition can the pair recruit outside resources?
flexible position near a vulnerable one discussion and support help is experienced as correction how does the recipient describe the effect?
strong position near a vulnerable one clarity and rapid impulse pressure, shame, or loss of agency voluntariness, right to stop, longitudinal outcome
mutual complementarity broad role distribution dependency and hidden hierarchy role rotation and reciprocity of cost
asymmetric chain clear hand-off of tasks or direction unequal burden both directions, power, and review rights

In Psychosophy the table concerns organizing action; in Temporistics it concerns organizing temporal and existential direction. The same positional pattern does not imply the same psychological mechanism.

4. Rival explanations #

Observed interaction may be better explained by experience, role allocation, culture, language, skill, health, resources, formal power, or conflict history. A typological hypothesis is useful only if it adds a distinguishable prediction beyond these factors.

5. Practical record #

Instead of “this pair is compatible,” record:

Proposed relation: [traditional name]
Observed episode: [specific action or information exchange]
Possible resource: [what may help the named task]
Possible friction: [what may hinder it]
Alternatives: [role, skill, context, power, resources]
Next check: [observation that could distinguish hypotheses]
Confidence: [low / limited / supported by repeated evidence]

Confidence describes the evidence for a specific claim, not the quality of the pair.
