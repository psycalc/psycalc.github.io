Christian Foundation of Family Discernment

Christian Foundation of Family Discernment #

Scope and status #

This page belongs to Christian Before We Build, the first developed application of the universal BWB research core. It explains how that application approaches family and marriage discernment. It does not define every family, require the universal core to adopt Christian theology, or treat a typological profile as a spiritual fact.

The application begins with Christ, Scripture, observable fruit, responsible conduct, prayer, church community, and mature counsel. Typologies may help formulate questions, but remain secondary research hypotheses.

Christian foundation #

Christian application claim: family discernment should attend to covenantal faithfulness, truth, responsibility, self-giving love, forgiveness, repair, and mutual service—not attraction or agreement alone.

Biblical grounding:

  • Genesis 2:24 and Matthew 19:4–6 frame marriage as a serious union.
  • 1 Corinthians 13:4–7 and Colossians 3:12–14 describe patient, truthful, forgiving love.
  • Ephesians 5:1–2 and Philippians 2:3–4 connect love with self-giving and concern for the other.
  • James 1:5 and Proverbs 11:14 commend wisdom and counsel in serious decisions.

These passages do not mean that professed faith, church participation, or a particular courtship process by itself produces a safe or lasting marriage.

Boundary with the universal core #

Universal BWB core Christian application
protects dignity, consent, safety, responsibility, reciprocity, repair, and non-negotiable boundaries grounds these commitments in a Christian account of creation, sin, grace, discipleship, and love
studies four compatibility levels for any pair of people applies them to Christian family discernment without making Christianity a typological level
treats context as a cross-cutting condition and safety as a constraint adds Scripture, observable fruit, prayer, church community, and mature counsel as normative resources
keeps the value-moral foundation distinct from strategy does not assign values to Temporistics; Temporistics remains the strategic time/existential hypothesis

Other worldview applications must name their own normative sources. The existence of such applications is not a Christian claim that all worldviews are equally true.

Why typologies remain secondary #

Socionics, Psychosophy, and Temporistics are non-equivalent heuristic models of proposed latent processes. They may organize questions about information exchange, joint action, and temporal direction. They do not reveal the heart, moral character, salvation, vocation, safety, or God's choice of a spouse.

Biblical grounding: Matthew 7:16–20 directs attention to fruit; 1 Samuel 16:7 warns against reducing a person to outward appearance; Galatians 5:22–23 names fruit of the Spirit rather than a profile.

Safety, forgiveness, and counsel #

Forgiveness does not require concealing abuse, abandoning a safety plan, removing consequences, or restoring trust without evidence of change. Church counsel should support truth, agency, accountability, and appropriate professional or legal help; it must not become coercion or surveillance.

If violence, coercive control, exploitation, threats, or inability to consent is present, safety takes precedence over typological conversation. A pastor, clinician, advocate, or legal professional contributes within their competence; no one profile replaces that work.

What cannot be inferred #

This page does not claim that:

  • Christian identity mechanically produces maturity or relationship success;
  • shared doctrine erases conflict, power differences, trauma, or practical incompatibility;
  • a church community is automatically wise or safe;
  • one type combination is more holy, marriageable, or destined than another;
  • any score, label, or human adviser can issue God's verdict about a relationship.

James 4:13–15 and Proverbs 16:9 caution against claiming control over outcomes.

Questions for discernment #

  • Which commitments do we both name, and what repeated conduct supports them?
  • How do we respond to truth, correction, refusal, and responsibility?
  • Can either person say no without punishment, spiritual pressure, or withdrawal of care?
  • How do we repair harm, seek counsel, and distinguish forgiveness from premature trust?
  • Which concern is a Christian normative question, which is contextual, and which is only a typological hypothesis?
